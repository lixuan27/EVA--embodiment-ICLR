\title{Actions as Effects: The Camera Frame as a Shared Action Interface for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Manipulation}

\author{Anonymous authors\\
Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A manipulation policy transfers to a new robot to the extent that its action carries nothing specific to the old one. We study this question for vision-language-action models on fixed-base arms with two-finger grippers, and we organise the study around the frame in which the action is expressed. The base frame stores a convention of the source robot, the gripper frame stores the geometry of its fingers, and the camera frame stores only the outcome that the images show. We describe actions by their motion effects, the image-plane displacements of the fingertips and pads of the gripper over the next fraction of a second, measured from anchors that follow analytically from the state and the kinematics of whichever robot is being controlled. A new robot brings its own anchors and decodes commands from the predicted effect through a closed-form geometric solve, so embodiment enters only at the input and output boundaries of the policy. To measure the value of this frame we build a controlled simulation benchmark with a procedurally generated family of 128 Franka-type source embodiments, eight commercially used targets held out from all training and grouped into appearance, gripper, arm and full changes, cameras aligned in a shared tabletop frame, a fixed pretraining budget and a protocol that separates targets absent from all training from targets seen during pretraining. Under the strict protocol the camera frame raises average task progress from 76.8 to 83.4 percent over the best gripper-frame policy and from 58.2 to 71.1 on full embodiment changes, outperforms language, waypoint and object-box auxiliary targets, and lets eight source embodiments match the transfer of 128. The prediction error of the effect on a target ranks the targets by their transfer, the removed failures concentrate in the grasp phase, and the same recipe improves established single-robot benchmarks and a physically different arm. The whole study fits sixteen GPUs.
\end{abstract}
